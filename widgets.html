What the numbers mean

Fitted line. The line ŷ = b₀ + b₁x that minimises the sum of squared residuals, where a residual is the vertical distance between a point and the line. Turn on “Show residuals” to see them.

R². The share of the variation in y that the line accounts for: 1 − (sum of squared residuals) ÷ (total sum of squares). For a straight-line fit it equals the square of the correlation r.

p-value. A two-sided t-test of the hypothesis that the true slope is zero, using t = b₁ ÷ SE(b₁) with n − 2 degrees of freedom. A small value means a slope this far from zero would be unlikely if x and y were unrelated. It needs at least three points.

Standard errors. The residual standard error is √(SSE ÷ (n − 2)), the typical size of a residual. The standard error of the slope divides it by √Σ(x − x̄)², so points spread widely along x pin the slope down more tightly.

Things to try. Load the four datasets of Anscombe’s quartet: their statistics are almost identical while the pictures differ completely. Then drag a single point far from the rest and watch how much one outlier can move the line.
